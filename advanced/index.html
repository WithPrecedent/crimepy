    Advanced User Guide¶

    This guide explains the NIBRS master files and every choice that crimepy makes in reading them, so that you can describe your data accurately and check crimepy's work.

    The NIBRS master files¶

    The FBI's Crime Data Explorer (CDE) publishes one master file of NIBRS data for each year from 1991. Each is a zip archive of one text file, from about 25 MB (1991) to about 600 MB (recent years) compressed, and over 6 GB of text for recent years. Every line is a record of one segment of a report, and the first two characters of a record are its segment level:

    Level Segment crimepy table
    BH (B1, B2, B3 before 2013) Batch header: one agency agencies
    01 Administrative: one incident administrative
    02 Offense offenses
    03 Property properties
    04 Victim victims
    05 Offender offenders
    06 Arrestee arrestees
    07 Group "B" Arrest Report group_b_arrests
    W1 Window exceptional clearance window_clearances
    W3 Window recovered property window_properties
    W6 Window arrestee window_arrestees

    The records are sorted by state and agency. Each agency's batch header comes first, followed by its incidents, each of which is a "record set" of its administrative segment and then its other segments in order of level.

    Where the fields are¶

    The positions of the fields come from the FBI's "NIBRS Flatfile Yearly Master Record Descriptions" (dated 12/18/95), which the CDE still distributes with the master files (crimepy.nibrs.cde.download_documentation downloads it). The document describes both fixed-length records (padded to 132 characters) and variable-length records (which end at their last field), and its positions are the same for both.

    The FBI added fields to the files after 1995 that the document does not describe. crimepy found them by profiling every position of the files from 2004 to 2025 and includes them:

    Segment Positions Field In the files crimepy column
    Administrative 89 Cargo theft (Y or N) 2013 and later cargo_theft
    Offense 60-67 Bias motivations #2 to #5 2013 to 2016 (later years drop them) bias_motivation_2 to bias_motivation_5
    Victim 124-135 The type of activity and assignment of a law enforcement officer who was a victim, and the ORI of another jurisdiction (handwritten into the document) Every year officer_activity, officer_assignment, officer_other_ori
    Offender 40 Ethnicity of the offender 2013 and later offender_ethnicity
    Batch header 285-292 A date that is the same for every agency in a state, which the FBI does not document 2013 to 2016 state_nibrs_date

    Fields that a year's records do not have are missing in that year.

    Batch headers¶

    Before 2013, each agency's batch header was split into three records: B1 (the agency's location and type), B2 (the populations of its first four counties), and B3 (its fifth county, the months it reported, and its FIPS county codes). From 2013, one BH record has all of them. crimepy joins the three records into one row, so the agencies table has the same columns in every year.

    The agencies table has, for each agency:

    • Its location: city, state, division, region, judicial_district, field_office, and, for each of up to five counties, ucr_county_1, msa_1, and fips_county_1 (and so on).
    • Its size and type: population_group, agency_type, core_city, and the current and previous census populations of each county (population_1 and last_population_1, and so on). population is the sum of the counties' populations.
    • Its reporting: date_ori_went_nibrs, nibrs_active, covered_by_ori (the agency that reports for it, if any), months_reported, and, for each month, whether it submitted a zero report (zero_report_jan), a Group "A" or "B" report (incident_report_jan), and a window record (window_report_jan). months_active is the number of months in which it submitted a zero report or a report of incidents.

    Linking the segments of an incident¶

    The segments of an incident share the agency's ORI and the incident number, and crimepy adds data_year (the year of the master file), so ["data_year", "ori", "incident_number"] (crimepy.nibrs.tables.KEYS) identifies an incident. Incident numbers are encrypted by the FBI in most years, so they cannot be linked to other records. A few incidents appear more than once in a file (193 of the 12.4 million in 2023, each an exact duplicate), and crimepy.nibrs.tables.incidents keeps the first.

    The FBI notes that the records after a batch header have the same first seven characters of the ORI as the header, but that the last two may differ. So crimepy adds agency_ori to every record: the ORI of the batch header that the record follows. Agencies are joined to incidents by agency_ori.

    A Group "B" Arrest Report has no incident, and its positions 14-25 are the arrest transaction number (arrest_transaction_number), not an incident number.

    Compression¶

    The CDE compressed the master files for 2017 to 2023 that it published before August 2026 with Deflate64, a variant of Deflate that Python's zipfile cannot read. crimepy reads those files with inflate64, without extracting them. The files that the CDE publishes now use Deflate, but copies downloaded earlier still work.

    How crimepy converts the data¶

    Kinds of fields¶

    Each field of a layout (crimepy.nibrs.layouts) has a kind, which decides what it becomes:

    Kind Becomes Example
    code a category (labeled if decode is true) location "20" becomes "Residence/Home"
    text text without blanks around it the ORI, incident number, or city
    int a whole number (Int64), missing if blank incident_hour, property_value
    date a date (datetime64), missing if blank or impossible incident_date ("20230101")
    bool true for "Y", false for "N", missing otherwise cargo_theft, core_city
    yes true if the field is a given character, false otherwise report_date ("R"), weapon_1_automatic ("A")
    quantity a number with thousandths drug_quantity_1 ("000000001250" becomes 1.25)

    Values with special meanings¶

    • Ages. Age codes are kept (as victim_age_code, and so on), and crimepy adds the age in years (victim_age): "NN" (under 24 hours), "NB" (1 to 6 days), and "BB" (7 to 364 days) are 0, "99" (99 or older) is 99, and "00" (unknown) is missing.
    • Offender sequence 0. An offender whose sequence number is 0 is unknown (nothing is known about the offender). incidents counts only known offenders in offender_count.
    • Report dates. If report_date is true, the incident date is the date that the incident was reported, because the date that it happened is not known.
    • Incident hour. A blank hour is unknown (missing), and an incident at midnight is in hour 0.
    • Property values. The FBI asks agencies to report a value of $1 when the value is unknown, so a value of 1 usually means unknown. crimepy keeps the reported value.
    • Automatic weapons. weapon_1_automatic is false when there is no weapon.

    Labels¶

    crimepy.nibrs.codes has the labels of every code, following the reference tables that the CDE publishes with its NIBRS downloads (such as NIBRS_OFFENSE_TYPE.csv) and, for fields that are only in the master files (such as the batch header's population group), the record descriptions. Codes changed over the years, and each codebook has every code that any year uses. A few codes meant something else in earlier years:

    • Race "A" was "Asian or Pacific Islander" before 2013, when "P" (Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander) was added.
    • Exceptional clearance "C" was "Extradition Denied" in the 1995 document and is "In Custody of Other Jurisdiction" now.

    A code that is not in a codebook (such as agency type "5" in some early years) is kept as it is, so nothing is lost. To use your own labels, pass them to crimepy.nibrs.decode:

    offenses = crimepy.nibrs.load('offenses', 2023, decode = False)
offenses = crimepy.nibrs.decode(
    offenses, ['location'], {'location': {'20': 'Home', '13': 'Street'}})

    offenses, arrestees, and group_b_arrests also get the category of the offense (offense_category, such as "Assault Offenses"), whom or what it is against (crime_against: "Person", "Property", "Society", or "Not a Crime"), and its group (offense_group: "A" or "B"), from the CDE's table of offenses (with the prefix "arrest_" for arrests). crimepy.nibrs.codes.OFFENSE_TYPES lists the offense codes that are against each of them, and those of a few types that cut across the categories ("National Security Offenses", "Rape Offenses", "Sex Offenses", and "Immigration Offenses"), such as for picking the crimes against property:

    property_crimes = crimepy.nibrs.codes.OFFENSE_TYPES['Property']

    Where the data are saved¶

    Everything is saved in the data folder: the CRIMEPY_DATA environment variable, if it is set, and otherwise the "crime" folder (crimepy.options.DATA_SUBFOLDER) of the data folder of amos's workspace. The workspace is the folder that holds the "code" folder above the current folder (or the folder named by the AMOS_WORKSPACE environment variable; see amos's Clerk), so the data are kept outside every project, and an amos project's results are kept beside them, in the workspace's "studies" folder. Change the data folder for a session with crimepy.options.DATA_FOLDER, or pass folder to any function.

    projects/                (amos's workspace)
    code/                (your projects' code)
    studies/             (the results of amos projects)
    data/
        crime/           (crimepy's data folder)
            nibrs/
                raw/
                    nibrs-2023.zip
                tables/
                    2023/
                        administrative.parquet
                        agencies.parquet
                        ...

    The parquet files have the codes (not the labels) and are compressed with zstd, so a recent year takes about 500 MB. They can be read by any tool that reads parquet (such as R's arrow or DuckDB). Delete a year's folder in "tables" to convert it again, or pass overwrite = True to crimepy.nibrs.convert.

    Memory¶

    crimepy parses a master file in chunks of a million records (crimepy.options.CHUNK_SIZE), so converting even the largest year needs little memory. A whole table of a recent year is large in memory, though: the 2023 administrative table has 12.4 million rows and takes about 1.6 GB. Pass states and columns to load to keep only what you need. They are applied while the parquet files are read, so the rest is never loaded.

    Datasets¶

    crimepy is built on amos, so its tables are amos.Datasets:

    • crimepy.nibrs.load, load_tables, read, and tables.build return datasets, whose data is the table (a pandas data frame). They take label, task, and groups, which amos models, splitters, and validators use. crimepy.nibrs.load_frame returns a plain data frame.
    • Every function of crimepy.analyze, crimepy.combine, and crimepy.plot (and crimepy.nibrs.decode) takes a dataset or a data frame.
    • The analyses and figures are amos techniques. A describer's table is kept in the dataset's tables and a figure in its figures, under the technique's name, and every step is recorded in its history. count makes a new dataset of counts, whose label is the counts.
    • crimepy.combine.stack and merge return a dataset with the label and groups of the first dataset given, if their columns are still there.

    A dataset gathers the data, what was learned from it, and how, so it is the record of an analysis. Any amos technique (a model, a validator, a metric, or a plot such as shap_beeswarm) can be applied to it directly, as in the tutorial.

    The amos techniques¶

    Importing crimepy adds these techniques to the amos library:

    Technique Kind What it does
    load_nibrs loader loads a table (or builds one) for years, with years, table, states, columns, decode, and folder parameters
    decode_nibrs munger replaces codes with labels
    count_crimes shaper counts rows by by (and periods of freq), so counts can be modeled
    crime_trend describer a table of counts over time
    population_rates describer counts per 100,000 people, from a table of counts
    crime_rates describer crimes per 100,000 people, using only agencies that reported all year (loading the agencies of the data's years and states)
    clearance_rates describer a table of clearance rates by group
    percent_change describer the change from one period to the next
    crosstab describer counts or shares of two columns against each other
    coverage describer the agencies that reported and the people they serve, from a table of agencies
    trend_plot, top_values, time_heatmap, age_pyramid plots the figures of crimepy.plot

    The functions of crimepy.analyze and crimepy.plot apply them. Name them in any worker of an amos settings file, with parameters in a {technique}_parameters section. See the amos documentation for the rest of a project's settings.
